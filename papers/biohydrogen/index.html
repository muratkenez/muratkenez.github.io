Cleaner production of biohydrogen using poplar leaves: Experimental and optimization studies

A. Yagmur Goren, Muratcan Kenez, Ibrahim Dincer and Ali Khalvati
Journal of Cleaner Production, vol. 434, article 140080 (1 January 2024).

Read the published paper: doi:10.1016/j.jclepro.2023.140080

What this paper does

  • Experimental study of dark fermentative hydrogen production from acid-pretreated poplar leaves using commercially available, inexpensive microorganism cultures in a purpose-built reactor.
  • Uses a six-factor Box-Behnken design to optimize the process; acid concentration was the most influential factor.
  • Reaches about 20 mg of hydrogen per gram of biomass at the optimum conditions.

Summary

This experimental study investigates dark fermentative hydrogen production from acid-pretreated poplar leaves, using commercially available and inexpensive microorganism cultures in a purpose-built reactor. A six-factor Box-Behnken design evaluates the effects of acid concentration, biomass amount, initial pH, temperature, mixing speed and microorganism amount on hydrogen yield. Acid concentration is the most influential parameter, and the optimized operating conditions give a maximum yield of about 20 mg of hydrogen per gram of biomass, indicating that poplar biomass with commercial cultures is a competitive route to biohydrogen.
