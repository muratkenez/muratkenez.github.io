Integrated direct air capture–to–ethylene production via CO2 electroreduction: Process modeling and techno-economic analysis

Murat Can Kenez and Gisele Azimi
Chemical Engineering Journal, vol. 542, article 177506 (15 August 2026). Open access (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0).

Read the published paper: doi:10.1016/j.cej.2026.177506

What this paper does

  • Builds the first integrated process model and techno-economic analysis of ethylene production from direct air capture (DAC) and alkaline MEA CO2 electrolysis, at 85 kt/yr scale.
  • Quantifies how carbonate crossover limits single-pass CO2 conversion (about 22%) and drives DAC oversizing (5.4x).
  • Reports a base-case minimum ethylene selling price of $8.58/kg, with DAC at 81% of equipment cost; material integration lowers it by 26% and genetic-algorithm optimization gives a further ~15%.
  • Outlines a technology roadmap toward $1.3-1.4/kg by 2040.

Abstract

Ethylene production from atmospheric CO2 through integrated direct air capture (DAC) and electrochemical CO2 reduction offers a pathway for atmospheric-carbon chemicals, but its commercial viability remains uncertain. Previous techno-economic studies have often treated the electrolyzer separately, assumed point-source CO2, or considered single-pass conversion as an independent parameter. These approaches overlook a key coupling in alkaline MEA electrolyzers: carbonate crossover through the anion exchange membrane limits single-pass conversion while also increasing the required upstream DAC capacity. The system-level economic impact of this coupling has not yet been quantified in the literature. This work presents a complete process model and techno-economic analysis of an integrated DAC–electrolyzer–separation plant producing 85 kt yr−1 of ethylene from atmospheric CO2. A system-level carbon utilization efficiency, SCUE, is introduced to link electrolyzer performance with DAC capacity. Under base-case conditions, SCUE is only 18.3%, with 64.1% of feed CO2 lost to crossover, single-pass conversion capped at 22%, and DAC capacity 5.4 times larger than the stoichiometric minimum. The minimum ethylene selling price (MESP) is $8.58/kg, dominated by electricity, which accounts for 56.7% of OPEX, and DAC, which accounts for 81% of equipment cost. Monte Carlo analysis (N = 10,000) gives a mean MESP of $8.78/kg, with an 80% confidence interval of $7.10–10.55/kg. Electricity price and crossover together explain 71.5% of the variance. Reducing the crossover coefficient from 0.5 to 0.1 decreases MESP by 23%. Independently, near-term material integration through anode CO2 recovery and O2 coupling to the DAC calciner lowers the base-case MESP by 26%, from $8.58 to $6.36/kg. Genetic-algorithm optimization over nine electrochemical variables yields a separate 15% reduction, from $8.77 to $7.49 kg−1.

Key findings

  • First integrated DAC–CO2 electrolysis–ethylene process model and techno-economic analysis (85 kt/yr ethylene).
  • Base-case minimum ethylene selling price (MESP): $8.58/kg, about 10× the fossil price; DAC is 81% of equipment cost.
  • Carbonate crossover caps single-pass conversion at about 22% and drives 5.4× DAC oversizing.
  • Material integration lowers MESP by 26% ($6.36/kg); genetic-algorithm optimization gives a separate ~15% reduction.
  • A technology roadmap indicates $1.3–1.4/kg is achievable by 2040 with coordinated advances.

Research questions addressed

  • What does ethylene from direct air capture and CO2 electrolysis cost at industrial scale?
  • How does carbonate crossover in an alkaline MEA electrolyzer affect upstream DAC sizing and plant economics?
  • Which process-integration and electrochemical parameters most influence the minimum ethylene selling price?
