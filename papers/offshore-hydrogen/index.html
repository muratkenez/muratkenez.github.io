Development and assessment of an offshore-based integrated hydrogen production and liquefaction system

Murat Can Kenez and Ibrahim Dincer
Applied Thermal Engineering, vol. 236, part D, article 121574 (10 January 2024).

Read the published paper: doi:10.1016/j.applthermaleng.2023.121574

Summary

This paper develops an integrated system that uses electricity from an offshore wind farm (the Hornsea Wind Farm in the North Sea) to produce hydrogen by electrolysis and then liquefy it. The overall system and its components are assessed with energy and exergy analyses based on balance equations, and parametric studies examine how operating conditions and design parameters affect the main performance indicators. Under the specified conditions, the overall energy efficiency is 70.45% and the overall exergy efficiency is 57.32%, with a specific energy consumption of the liquefaction plant of 62.24 kWh/kg of liquid hydrogen. The levelized cost of hydrogen for the plant is $7.28/kg. The analysis also identifies the main sources of exergy destruction, pointing to opportunities for improving the system.

Key results

  • Overall energy efficiency: 70.45%
  • Overall exergy efficiency: 57.32%
  • Specific energy consumption of the liquefaction plant: 62.24 kWh/kg-LH2
  • Levelized cost of hydrogen: $7.28/kg
