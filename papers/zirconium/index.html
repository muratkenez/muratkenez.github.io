Plasma processing as green alternative for advanced treatment of zirconium minerals: environmental and economic impact assessment

Dmytro Yelatontsev, Murat Kenez, Anatolii Mukhachev and Gisele Azimi
Chemical Engineering Journal, vol. 548, article 181701 (November 2026). Review article. Open access (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0).

Read the published paper: doi:10.1016/j.cej.2026.181701

What this paper does

  • Reviews plasma-based routes (thermal arc, inductively coupled and hybrid) for decomposing zircon and compares them with conventional routes such as carbochlorination, fluorosilicate fusion and alkali fusion.
  • Provides a techno-economic assessment and a cradle-to-gate life-cycle assessment on a basis of 1 kg of ZrO2.
  • Reports up to 80% lower greenhouse-gas emissions (1.9 vs 8.4 kg CO2-eq per kg) with low-carbon electricity, and a projected break-even price of 5.27 USD/kg at industrial scale (more than 100 t/day).
  • Identifies electrode erosion and silica removal as the main engineering bottlenecks.

Abstract

Amidst the rising demand for nuclear energy and the urgent need to decarbonize critical metal supply chains, conventional zirconium production remains energy-intensive and waste-heavy. This review critically evaluates plasma-based pathways, including thermal-arc, inductively coupled, and hybrid systems, using a comparative framework of process intensification metrics and sustainability indicators. We present a techno-economic assessment and life-cycle assessment on a cradle-to-gate basis (1 kg ZrO2). Results show that plasma processing achieves extreme intensification, with volumetric productivities (~15,000 kg·h−1·m−3) and residence times (10–100 ms) outperforming conventional alkali fusion by 3–4 orders of magnitude. Environmentally, plasma processing reduces greenhouse-gas emissions by up to 80% (1.9 vs 8.4 kg CO2-eq kg−1) when integrated with low-carbon electricity. Economically, it reaches cost parity with commercial benchmarks at industrial scales (>100 t/day), with a projected break-even price of 5.27 USD/kg. However, competitiveness remains sensitive to electricity pricing, reactor durability, and the utilization of silicon-bearing coproducts (e.g., SiCl4, SiC, SiO2), which significantly influence the net process economics. This study provides a strategic roadmap for researchers and industry, identifying electrode erosion and SiO2 removal as the primary engineering bottlenecks. By bridging the gap between laboratory metrics and industrial requirements, it provides a decision-making framework for prioritizing research and investment toward the electrified future of zirconium metallurgy.

Key findings

  • Thermal plasma dissociates zircon in one stage, removing silica in flight; ultra-fast quenching stabilizes fine plasma-dissociated zircon and suppresses back-oxidation.
  • Plasma processing reduces greenhouse-gas emissions by up to 80% (1.9 vs 8.4 kg CO2-eq per kg ZrO2) with low-carbon electricity.
  • Cost parity with commercial benchmarks at industrial scale (>100 t/day); projected break-even price of 5.27 USD/kg.
  • TEA and LCA results are most sensitive to electricity price, CAPEX and coproduct utilization.
  • Reactor linings, SiO2 removal and Zr/Hf separation remain the key bottlenecks.
